STACK

P24 · Ergonomic kitchenware system
Academic product design project based on Rigtig’s design language

01 — CONTEXT

A COHERENT FAMILY, NOT ISOLATED OBJECTS.

STACK explores how ergonomics, functionality and brand identity can be translated into a coherent family of kitchen objects: a water jug and a series of storage containers.

One formal language connects different functions.

02 — FORM EXPLORATION

SOFT GEOMETRY. REPEATED PROPORTIONS. MATERIAL CONTRAST.

The collection develops simple cylindrical volumes with rounded transitions. Repetition creates rhythm and a recognisable family, while transparent bodies, neutral finishes and wooden elements introduce a restrained domestic character.

03 — ERGONOMICS + SYSTEM

USE IS BUILT INTO THE FORM.

Ergonomics is considered through proportion, grip and handling. The jug studies hand position and pouring, while the container and lid consider everyday opening, closing and manipulation. Repeated cylindrical volumes allow the pieces to be visually combined and stacked as a system.

04 — WATER JUG

A VERTICAL SEQUENCE OF TRANSPARENT VOLUMES.

The water jug is built from a sequence of rounded transparent volumes. Its segmented construction gives the object a distinctive identity while preserving a simple silhouette. The upper element incorporates the pouring area without breaking the continuity of the system.

05 — STORAGE

MATERIAL CONTRAST AT A DOMESTIC SCALE.

The storage containers repeat the same rounded geometry in a compact format. A wooden lid introduces a tactile contrast with the body and reinforces the family through proportion and stacking.

06 — TECHNICAL DEFINITION

FROM FORM TO DEFINITION.

Technical drawings define the principal proportions, sections, radii, wall thicknesses and connection details of the jug and storage container.

07 — RESULT

ONE LANGUAGE. ONE COLLECTION.

Proportion, repetition and material contrast create coherence across the family.
