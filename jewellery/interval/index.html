INTERVAL

P25 · WATCH-JEWEL · RUBY IN ZOISITE · HANDWORK · CONCEPTUAL PROPOSAL

We usually understand time as something continuous. An ordered succession of seconds, hours and days that we try to measure, control and organise. INTERVAL begins when that continuity breaks.

The starting point is a piece of ruby in zoisite, cut, shaped and polished by hand. Its outer surface is clean and controlled, but a fracture divides the stone in two. Instead of repairing the accident or trying to hide it, I decided to keep it.

Because that was precisely where the interesting part lay.

The fracture stopped being the end of the piece and became the beginning of the design. The watch is built around that void: the hands are born from the space between the two stones, and time appears exactly where matter stops being perfect.

The piece speaks of our constant need for control. To arrive on time, to organise everything and to keep a rhythm we cannot always sustain. Against that precision appears the fracture: unexpected, irregular and impossible to control.

The bracelet carries that tension into the metal. A tangle of interwoven wires represents paths that cross, overlap and lose any linear trajectory. In this way, the piece sets order against chaos, precision against accident, polished surface against broken matter.

INTERVAL does not try to repair the break.
It uses it to stop the gaze.

Because perhaps we need something to break in order to remember that time does not always have to move in a straight line.

TIME BEGINS IN THE BREAK.

Portfolio note: The stone shown in the photographs is a physical piece made by hand. The complete watch, mechanism and bracelet are a visual development proposal for the concept.
