LATENT

2025 · ROCK CRYSTAL RING · HAND-FABRICATED · CONTROLLED FRACTURE

01 — CONCEPT

I DIDN'T WANT TO REPAIR THE FRACTURES. I WANTED MORE.

The project began with a raw piece of rock crystal and a simple reversal: instead of treating internal fractures as damage, I wanted them to become an active part of the ring. Not simply accepted, but deliberately intensified.

Control is used to create what control would usually prevent.

02 — PROCESS

FROM RAW CRYSTAL TO CONTROLLED FRACTURE.

The ring was cut from raw rock crystal, shaped and drilled, faceted to create controlled planes, subjected to controlled thermal contrasts to encourage internal fractures, and finally polished to clarify the exterior while keeping the internal discontinuities visible.

03 — MATERIAL AS INTERFACE

THE EVENT HAPPENS INSIDE.

The facets give the outside of the ring a deliberate geometry. The fractures disrupt that order from within. Reflection and refraction across polished planes and internal discontinuities create shifting highlights as the piece moves.

The optical effect is not an applied finish. It is a direct consequence of the material transformation.

04 — CONTROLLED INTERRUPTION

KNOWING THE MATERIAL WELL ENOUGH TO DISTURB IT.

Previous engineering training influenced the approach to crystal: not as a passive surface, but as a material whose behaviour could inform the design decision. The intervention is deliberate, while the exact internal path of every fracture remains partly uncertain.

05 — OBJECT

LATENT / ROCK CRYSTAL RING

Material: rock crystal. Process: cutting, shaping, vertical drilling, faceting, controlled thermal contrast, fracturing and polishing. Authorship: 100% designed and fabricated by Natalia Antelo Cubría.

The fracture is not what remains after the design. It is part of the design.
