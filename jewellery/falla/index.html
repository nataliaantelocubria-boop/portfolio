FALLA

2025

Some stones are valued for their purity, their symmetry, or for how closely they approach an idea of perfection. FALLA was born from looking in exactly the opposite direction.

While working with agate, fractures, veins, variations in colour and irregular shapes stopped being seen as flaws. They were precisely what made each fragment different. No two stones could ever be exactly the same, and imperfection therefore became identity. Cutting and polishing do not seek to erase the natural characteristics of the agate, but to work with them and make them visible.

From there, the piece is built around contrast. At its centre is the most irregular agate: visually fractured, rough and almost entirely raw. It is neither hidden nor corrected. Instead, it becomes the focal point. Brass supports it through a visible structure, while on both sides the composition gradually becomes more ordered through rings, settings and agate discs.

The process is also part of the story. The agates were cut, shaped and polished by hand, preserving their natural differences, while brass acts as both a connecting and structural element.

FALLA is not really about a perfect stone.
It is about what we would normally try to correct.
Because perhaps a flaw is not something that needs to be hidden.
Perhaps it is precisely what makes something unique.
